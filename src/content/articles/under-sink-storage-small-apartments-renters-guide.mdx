The under-sink cabinet is the worst-designed storage in a small apartment: a big box with pipes running through the middle, a garbage disposal taking up a corner, and a dark interior where cleaning supplies go to be forgotten. It is also the most fixable. With a tension rod, a few bins, and a door rack, this cabinet can hold every cleaning supply you own — without a single hole drilled. This guide covers under sink storage for small apartments the renter's way: everything removable, everything around the pipes. The pillar guide to [renter-friendly kitchen storage ideas for small apartments](/kitchen-storage/renter-friendly-kitchen-storage-ideas-small-apartments/) covers the whole kitchen if you want the wider picture.

Start by emptying the cabinet completely and wiping it down. Check for leaks while it is empty — run the tap, look at every pipe joint, and feel the cabinet floor for dampness. If you find a leak, report it to your landlord before organizing anything; a leak is their repair, not yours.

## 1. A Tension Rod for Spray Bottles

The single most effective under-sink fix costs a few dollars. A tension rod wedged across the cabinet lets you hang spray bottles by their trigger handles, keeping them upright, visible, and off the floor.

**Required space:** Two parallel cabinet walls 10–28 inches (25–71 cm) apart, with the rod placed high enough that bottles hang clear of the cabinet floor.
**Installation:** Twist to extend until the rod grips both walls firmly. Test it by pressing down in the middle before hanging anything.
**Approximate dimensions:** Short rods span 10–18 inches (25–46 cm); longer ones reach 18–28 inches (46–71 cm). Standard spray bottles are about 10–12 inches (25–30 cm) tall, so the rod needs roughly 14 inches (36 cm) of clearance below it.
**Rental-friendly limits:** None — tension alone holds it. Keep the load light; a rod at full extension holds less than one near its minimum.
**Pros:** Frees the entire cabinet floor, keeps bottles visible, and installs in under a minute.
**Cons:** Slips if overloaded or if the cabinet walls are not parallel; check it monthly.
**Suitable products:** Spring tension rods, short shower-curtain rods.

## 2. Stackable Bins on the Cabinet Floor

With the spray bottles hanging, the floor is free for stackable bins. One bin for dish supplies, one for surface cleaners, one for trash bags and sponges — pull a bin out like a drawer instead of reaching past everything.

**Required space:** A cabinet floor area at least 10 inches (25 cm) deep, measured around the pipes.
**Installation:** None — place and fill. Put the most-used bin at the front.
**Approximate dimensions:** Bins commonly run 8–12 inches (20–30 cm) long, 6–8 inches (15–20 cm) wide, and 6–8 inches (15–20 cm) tall. Measure the height under the pipes before buying.
**Rental-friendly limits:** None. Use bins with handles so you can pull them out without knocking things over.
**Pros:** Turns a deep, dark cabinet into pull-out drawers, and bins contain leaks if a bottle tips.
**Cons:** Bins waste a little space around the pipes; measure carefully or you will buy bins that do not fit.
**Suitable products:** Clear stackable bins with handles, small plastic crates, waterproof trays.

## 3. A Door Rack for Sponges and Small Items

The inside of the under-sink door is prime space for the small things that vanish: sponges, scrub brushes, gloves, and dishwasher tablets. A narrow rack that hooks over the door or sticks on with adhesive keeps them upright and dry.

**Required space:** A door interior at least 10 inches (25 cm) wide, with clearance for the rack's depth when the door closes.
**Installation:** Hook over the door, or attach with adhesive strips on a clean, dry surface.
**Approximate dimensions:** Door racks are commonly 8–12 inches (20–30 cm) wide and 10–14 inches (25–36 cm) tall.
**Rental-friendly limits:** Check the rack does not hit the pipes or the bins when the door closes. Adhesive on the door is fine if the surface is smooth and clean.
**Pros:** Frees shelf and floor space, and sponges dry faster hanging than sitting in a damp pile.
**Cons:** A rack loaded with wet sponges can drip — put a small tray or absorbent mat on the cabinet floor beneath it.
**Suitable products:** Over-door wire racks, adhesive sponge holders, small adhesive hooks for gloves. 3M's [Command strips](https://www.3m.com/3M/en_US/command/) work well for lightweight door racks on smooth surfaces.

## 4. A Waterproof Tray or Mat Under Everything

This is not storage — it is insurance. A shallow waterproof tray or an absorbent mat under the pipes catches drips, contains a leaking bottle, and protects the cabinet floor from the damage landlords charge for.

**Required space:** The cabinet floor, typically 18–24 inches (46–61 cm) wide and 18–22 inches (46–56 cm) deep, minus the pipes.
**Installation:** Lay it flat on the cabinet floor. Cut a flexible mat around the pipes with scissors if needed.
**Approximate dimensions:** Trays commonly run 16–22 inches (41–56 cm) wide and 14–20 inches (36–51 cm) deep; mats can be trimmed to fit.
**Rental-friendly limits:** None — it protects the cabinet, which landlords appreciate.
**Pros:** A $10 tray can prevent a repair bill many times its cost, and it makes cleaning up a spill a one-minute job.
**Cons:** Trays take up a little vertical space, so measure the height under the pipes first.
**Suitable products:** Waterproof under-sink trays, absorbent mats, shallow plastic boot trays cut to size. For more under-sink ideas, [The Spruce](https://www.thespruce.com/) covers sink cabinet organization for small kitchens.

## 5. A Small Freestanding Shelf Around the Pipes

A two-tier shelf unit sized to fit around the pipes adds a second level inside the cabinet — but only if you measure the pipes first. The shelf must sit beside or below the pipes, not through them.

**Required space:** A clear pocket of cabinet at least 10 inches (25 cm) wide and 10 inches (25 cm) deep, with enough vertical clearance for two tiers.
**Installation:** Assemble and place — no fixing needed. Some shelf units are expandable and can be adjusted to fit around pipes.
**Approximate dimensions:** Small under-sink shelves are typically 10–16 inches (25–41 cm) wide, 10–14 inches (25–36 cm) deep, and 12–16 inches (30–41 cm) tall.
**Rental-friendly limits:** None — freestanding. Never force a shelf against a pipe; pressure on a joint can cause a leak.
**Pros:** Doubles the usable space in a cabinet that otherwise wastes its upper half.
**Cons:** The pipes make the fit fiddly, and a shelf that is slightly too big is useless — measure twice.
**Suitable products:** Expandable under-sink shelf units, small stackable shelf risers.

For more on choosing shelves that do not need a drill, see the [no-drill shelves comparison](/renter-friendly-solutions/no-drill-shelves-apartment-walls-compared/), and the [no-drill kitchen storage guide](/kitchen-storage/no-drill-kitchen-storage-for-renters/) covers tension rods and adhesive options in depth.

## 6. A Pull-Out Caddy for Daily Cleaning Supplies

If you reach for dish soap, a sponge, and an all-purpose spray every day, keep them in a small caddy with a handle. Carry it to the sink, use it, put it back — no more reaching into the dark cabinet three times a day.

**Required space:** A cabinet floor spot about 8–12 inches (20–30 cm) wide, or a spot beside the sink.
**Installation:** None — place and fill.
**Approximate dimensions:** Caddies are commonly 8–12 inches (20–30 cm) long, 6–8 inches (15–20 cm) wide, and 6–8 inches (15–20 cm) tall.
**Rental-friendly limits:** None.
**Pros:** The most-used supplies are always together and always reachable, and the caddy contains any drips.
**Cons:** A caddy takes up floor space, so keep it small and only include daily-use items.
**Suitable products:** Plastic caddies with handles, small cleaning supply totes.

## 7. Label Everything and Keep a Spare Stock Zone

The under-sink cabinet becomes a black hole when every bottle looks the same. Label bins by category — dishes, surfaces, laundry, trash bags — and keep one small zone at the back for spare refills so you stop buying duplicates.

**Required space:** One bin or one shelf section at the back of the cabinet for spares.
**Installation:** None — labels on bins, and a simple rule: spares live at the back, daily items at the front.
**Approximate dimensions:** A spare-stock bin of about 8–10 inches (20–25 cm) wide holds most refill bottles.
**Rental-friendly limits:** None.
**Pros:** You can see what you have, you stop buying a third bottle of dish soap, and restocking takes seconds.
**Cons:** Labels need replacing if bins get wet — use waterproof labels or a marker on tape.
**Suitable products:** Waterproof labels, label maker tape, clear bins.

## Common Mistakes

**Storing food under the sink.** The cabinet is damp, dark, and next to the drain — a bad home for food. Cleaning supplies only; food belongs in the pantry system described in the [small apartment pantry guide](/kitchen-storage/small-apartment-pantry-organization-no-pantry/).

**Ignoring a slow leak.** A damp cabinet floor left for months rots the wood and becomes a repair charge at move-out. Check the pipes monthly and report any leak to the landlord the day you find it.

**Buying storage before measuring around the pipes.** The pipes eat more space than you expect. Measure the clear pockets between and beside them before buying a single bin or shelf.

**Overloading the tension rod.** A rod holding eight heavy spray bottles will slip eventually, and the crash will soak everything below. Keep it to four or five light bottles, and check the grip monthly.

**No tray under liquids.** One tipped bottle of bleach or drain cleaner can stain or damage a cabinet floor permanently. A tray is the cheapest insurance in the whole kitchen.

## Conclusion

The under-sink cabinet does not need more space — it needs the space it has used properly. The single best first step is a tension rod for spray bottles plus one waterproof tray on the cabinet floor: roughly $15–$25, installed in ten minutes, and enough to transform the messiest cabinet in your kitchen. From there, bins, a door rack, and a small shelf build on the same system.
