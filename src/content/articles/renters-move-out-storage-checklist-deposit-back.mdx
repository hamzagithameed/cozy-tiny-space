Moving out is when all those clever no-drill storage solutions come due. Every adhesive hook, tension rod, leaning shelf and over-door rack has to come down — and come down cleanly, or the deposit pays for it. This renter move out deposit checklist storage guide walks you room by room through what to remove, how to remove it without damage, how to fix the small marks that do happen, and how to document everything so your deposit comes back in full. If you are still setting up storage in your next place, the method guide to start with is [no-drill shelves for apartment walls: every method compared](/renter-friendly-solutions/no-drill-shelves-apartment-walls-compared/).

Start two weeks before moving day, not the night before. Removal is slow work when it is done properly, and rushing it is exactly how paint comes off. Work one room at a time, keep a small repair kit beside you, and photograph each wall as you finish it.

## What You Need Before You Start

**Required space:** Clear floor space in each room so you can reach every wall, plus a work surface for the repair kit.

**Installation:** Not applicable — this is a removal guide. Gather your tools first: a hair dryer, dental floss or fishing line, rubbing alcohol, warm soapy water, a soft cloth, spackle and a putty knife, fine sandpaper, touch-up paint, felt pads for the next apartment, and your phone for photos.

**Approximate dimensions:** A basic repair kit fits in a shoebox. Touch-up paint is usually sold in small 8 oz (237 ml) pots, which is enough for dozens of small marks.

**Rental-friendly limits:** Never use a razor blade or a metal scraper on a painted wall — they gouge the surface and turn a small fix into a real repair. Dental floss and heat are the safe tools.

**Pros:** Doing removal yourself costs almost nothing and gives you control over the result. A landlord's contractor charges far more than a pot of touch-up paint.

**Cons:** It takes time — budget 3–5 hours across the whole apartment, spread over several days.

**Suitable products:** A hair dryer, dental floss, rubbing alcohol, spackle, fine sandpaper (220 grit), and touch-up paint in your wall colour. If you do not know the colour, ask your landlord for the code before you buy paint.

## Kitchen: Hooks, Racks and Under-Sink Storage

The kitchen usually has the most adhesive in the apartment: spice racks, towel hooks, under-cabinet rails and under-sink organizers.

**Required space:** Clear the countertops so you can reach every wall and cabinet interior.

**Installation:** Remove adhesive hooks and racks using the pull-tab method — hold the base against the wall, pull the tab straight down slowly, parallel to the wall, until it releases. Take down tension rods by twisting them shorter until the pressure releases, then lift them out. Pull the under-sink rolling cart out and wipe the cabinet floor.

**Approximate dimensions:** Kitchen adhesive items are usually small to medium hooks of 1–3 in (2.5–8 cm). Check each one for residue after removal.

**Rental-friendly limits:** Grease is the kitchen's special problem — adhesive residue mixes with cooking grease and is harder to see. Wipe every spot with warm soapy water after removal, then with rubbing alcohol if residue remains. Check inside cabinets for shelf liner you added, and peel it off slowly — old liner can leave sticky patches.

**Pros:** Kitchen walls are often tile, which cleans up perfectly. Painted kitchen walls need the same care as any other room.

**Cons:** Grease makes residue harder to remove, and spice racks above the stove may have heat-weakened adhesive that leaves more residue than usual.

**Suitable products:** Rubbing alcohol for residue, warm soapy water for grease, and touch-up paint for any marks. ## Bedroom and Closet: Shelves, Rods and Hanging Organizers

The bedroom and closet hold the most storage: leaning shelves, closet rods, hanging organizers, adhesive hooks and under-bed units.

**Required space:** Empty the closet completely before you start — you cannot check the walls behind clothes.

**Installation:** Take leaning shelves apart or carry them out whole, and check the wall where the top leaned for rub marks — a quick wipe usually fixes them. Twist tension rods shorter to release them, and check the rod ends' contact points for pressure marks. Remove hanging organizers from closet rods and adhesive hooks from the closet walls and door. Pull under-bed storage out and vacuum the floor beneath it.

**Approximate dimensions:** Closet tension rods span 24–48 in (61–122 cm) and leave two small contact points per rod. Leaning shelves leave a rub line about as wide as the unit, usually 24–36 in (61–91 cm).

**Rental-friendly limits:** Pressure marks from tension rods are the most common closet issue. Light marks often disappear with a wipe of warm soapy water. Deeper dents may need a small amount of spackle. Check the closet floor for scuffs from rolling carts and hampers, and the door for scuffs from over-door hooks.

**Pros:** Closet walls are usually plain painted drywall, which is the easiest surface to repair. Most closet marks are small and quick to fix.

**Cons:** Closets are easy to forget — renters often leave a hook or a rod behind a stack of boxes. Do the closet last, when it is fully empty, and check every wall with a flashlight.

**Suitable products:** Spackle and sandpaper for dents, touch-up paint, and a flashlight for the final check. For the closet systems you are removing, see [small bedroom closet storage for renters: the complete guide](/bedroom-closet-storage/small-bedroom-closet-storage-renters-complete-guide/).

## Bathroom: Suction, Adhesive and Tension Storage

The bathroom has suction shelves, waterproof adhesive hooks, tension pole caddies and over-door towel racks — and humidity changes how they come off.

**Required space:** Clear the shower, the sink area and the back of the door so every surface is reachable.

**Installation:** Flip the levers on suction cups and lift them off — they should leave no marks on tile or glass. Remove waterproof adhesive hooks with the pull-tab method, warming the strip with a hair dryer on low if it resists. Shorten tension pole caddies from the ceiling down and lift them out. Take over-door racks off and check the door's top edge for scuffs.

**Approximate dimensions:** Suction shelves are 8–16 in (20–41 cm) wide; tension poles extend 60–96 in (152–244 cm). Check every contact point, including the ceiling where a pole caddy pressed.

**Rental-friendly limits:** Tile and glass are forgiving — adhesive removes cleanly from both. Painted bathroom walls are less forgiving because steam softens paint over time, so pull tabs extra slowly here. Check for mould or mildew behind shower caddies and clean it before the inspection.

**Pros:** Most bathroom storage is on tile, which is the easiest surface to restore. Suction products leave nothing behind at all.

**Cons:** Steam-softened paint peels more easily than dry-room paint, and mould behind a caddy can look like damage if it is not cleaned.

**Suitable products:** Rubbing alcohol for tile residue, a mould cleaner for behind-caddy spots, and touch-up paint for painted walls. The bathroom setups are covered in [renter-friendly bathroom storage ideas for tiny bathrooms](/bathroom-storage/renter-friendly-bathroom-storage-ideas-tiny-bathrooms/).

## Living Room and Entryway: Shelves, Hooks and Freestanding Units

The living room and entryway have the most visible walls, so this is where the inspection will look hardest. Leaning shelves, adhesive picture ledges, entryway hooks and freestanding bookcases all live here.

**Required space:** Move furniture away from every wall so you can see and reach the full surface.

**Installation:** Remove picture ledges and frames by pulling each strip's tab slowly — reach behind the frame to find every tab, since a missed tab tears paint when the frame is pulled away. Take down entryway hooks with the pull-tab method. Move freestanding shelves and check the floor beneath them for scuffs, and the wall behind them for dust outlines — a wipe with a damp cloth removes most dust marks.

**Approximate dimensions:** Picture ledges run 12–24 in (30–61 cm) wide with two to four strips each. Entryway hooks are 1–4 in (2.5–10 cm) tall. Check the wall behind every unit, not just where the strips were.

**Rental-friendly limits:** Dust outlines behind shelves are common and usually wipe off — do not mistake them for damage, and do not let an inspector mistake them either. Clean them before the inspection. Check the entryway door for scuffs from over-door hooks and the floor for marks from shoe racks.

**Pros:** Living room walls are usually in the best condition because they are dry and low-humidity. Most marks here are dust, not damage.

**Cons:** This is the room the inspector sees first, so any missed mark matters more here. Take your time and photograph every wall.

**Suitable products:** A damp cloth for dust outlines, rubbing alcohol for residue, and touch-up paint. For the entryway setup, review [rental apartment entryway storage ideas for small spaces](/living-room-entryway-storage/rental-apartment-entryway-storage-ideas-small-spaces/).

## Laundry Area: Racks, Carts and Curtains

The laundry nook has a drying rack, a rolling cart, a tension-rod shelf and possibly a curtain — all of which come down quickly, but each leaves something to check.

**Required space:** Clear the machines and the floor so you can reach the walls and the ceiling above the nook.

**Installation:** Fold the drying rack flat and carry it out. Roll the cart out and wipe the floor beneath it. Shorten tension rods to release them and check the contact points. Take the curtain down and remove the rod. Wipe the shelf area and check for vibration marks above the machines.

**Approximate dimensions:** Drying racks are 24–30 in (61–76 cm) wide; tension-rod shelves span 24–48 in (61–122 cm). Check the wall above the washer for scuffs from a shelf that vibrated against it.

**Rental-friendly limits:** Vibration marks above the washer are the laundry area's special issue — a shelf that rattled against the wall can leave a rub line. A damp cloth usually removes it; spackle and paint fix deeper marks.

**Pros:** Laundry areas are small, so this room is the fastest to clear. Most items here are freestanding or tension-mounted, with no adhesive at all.

**Cons:** The area is easy to rush because it is small — slow down and check the wall above the machines, which is the spot most renters miss.

**Suitable products:** A damp cloth, rubbing alcohol, and touch-up paint. The laundry setups are in [small apartment laundry storage solutions for renters](/renter-friendly-solutions/small-apartment-laundry-storage-solutions-renters/).

## Repairing Small Marks

Small marks are normal, and fixing them is straightforward. For a scuff or a dust outline, wipe with warm soapy water and let it dry. For a small paint peel or a dent, clean the area, press a small amount of spackle into the damage with a putty knife, let it dry fully, sand it smooth with 220-grit sandpaper, wipe away the dust, and touch up with matching paint using a small brush. [Family Handyman](https://www.familyhandyman.com/) has step-by-step guidance on patching small drywall holes and dents if you have never done it before. For adhesive residue, rub with warm soapy water first, then rubbing alcohol if the residue persists. For a pressure mark from a tension rod, try a damp cloth first — light marks often vanish. If the dent remains, treat it like any small dent: spackle, sand, paint. Photograph each repair after it dries so you have a record.

## The Final Walkthrough

On the last day, after everything is out and the apartment is clean, walk through every room with your phone and photograph every wall, every door and every floor in good light. Open every closet and cabinet and photograph inside them. Compare your photos with the ones you took when you moved in, if you have them. [Apartment Therapy](https://www.apartmenttherapy.com/) has a practical move-out cleaning checklist that pairs well with this removal checklist. If you find a mark you missed, fix it now — you still have time before the inspection. Leave the apartment clean, leave the keys as instructed, and keep your photos for at least a year after the deposit is returned, in case of a dispute. ## Common Mistakes

**Leaving removal to the last night.** Rushed removal peels paint. Start two weeks early and do one room at a time.

**Pulling adhesive tabs toward you.** Pulling away from the wall peels paint. Pull straight down, slowly, parallel to the wall — every time.

**Forgetting the closet.** The closet is the most commonly missed room because it is checked while still full. Empty it completely, then check every wall with a flashlight.

**Hiding damage instead of fixing it.** A small paint peel fixed with spackle and touch-up paint costs a few dollars. The same peel found by an inspector costs far more.

**Skipping the photos.** Without photos, a deposit dispute is your word against the landlord's. Photograph every room after cleaning, in good light, and keep the photos.

## Conclusion

Getting your deposit back is mostly a matter of patience: remove everything slowly, fix the small marks, and photograph the result. The single best first step is to start two weeks early with the room that has the most adhesive — usually the kitchen — and work through the apartment one room at a time. Your next landlord will never know the shelves were there, and your deposit will come back in full.
